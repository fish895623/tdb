## The commercial license: contribute or pay, every six months

A commercial license is a short written agreement with the copyright holder. It
is **not** granted by this page or by sending a pull request; register first
(see "How to get one").

It is granted in **six-month periods**. To have it for a period, the company
does **one** of these:

- **Contribute.** At least one qualifying pull request from the company is
  accepted into this repository during the period. See "What qualifies".
- **Pay.** Pay the fee for the period. The fee is set in the written agreement.

Either one is enough. A company may contribute in one period and pay in the
next.

**While the license is active**, the company may use, modify, embed and
distribute tdb, and run it as a service, without the AGPL's source-sharing
obligations.

**If a period ends with neither a qualifying contribution nor payment**, the
commercial license does not renew. The company can still use tdb under the AGPL,
and can reinstate the commercial license by contributing or paying. What happens
to copies already shipped under an active license is set in the written
agreement.

### What qualifies as a contribution

- The pull request is authored by the company's employees or contractors, and is
  **accepted (merged)** into the main branch.
- It is **meaningful**: a bug fix with a test, a feature or improvement the
  maintainers agreed to, additional test coverage, or a substantial documentation
  change. Typo fixes, formatting or whitespace changes, dependency bumps and
  generated changes do not count.
- The contributor has signed the contributor agreement in
  [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), so the project can include the change under
  both licenses.
- The maintainers decide whether it qualifies, in good faith. To avoid a
  rejected pull request costing a license period, open an issue first so the
  scope is agreed before the work is done.

agreement sets the fee, the start of the first period, and the contact who
submits the pull requests.
